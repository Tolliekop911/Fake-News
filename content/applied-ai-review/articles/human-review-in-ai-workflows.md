"Human in the loop" is one of the most repeated phrases in AI, and one of the least examined. A review step that nobody actually performs is worse than no claim of review at all. Designing it well is the difference between a safe workflow and a theatrical one.

## What good review is for

Human review exists to catch the AI's predictable failures — hallucinations, omissions, missing context — before they become consequential. In documentation, it is where accountability for the record stays with a qualified person.

## The trap: review that erases the savings

If checking an AI draft takes as long as writing from scratch, the tool has saved nothing. The design goal is review that is **fast but real**: the reviewer can verify and correct quickly, without being lulled into rubber-stamping.

## What makes review effective

- **Surface the AI's reasoning or sources** so the reviewer checks, not re-derives.
- **Highlight uncertain or inferred content** rather than presenting everything as equally confident.
- **Make correction trivial** — easy edits mean edits actually happen.
- **Preserve the reviewer's authority** to reject the draft entirely.

## The automation-complacency problem

The better an AI gets, the more tempting it is to stop checking — right when a rare error slips through. Good workflows resist this by keeping the reviewer engaged: variable checks, visible uncertainty, and accountability that stays with the human.

## Measure the real cost

Track review time and correction rate alongside the drafting time saved. The honest productivity number is net of review. A tool that looks fast but requires heavy checking may be slower than it appears.
