The monthly price a vendor quotes is rarely what you end up paying. Comparing practice software on sticker price alone is how clinics end up surprised. A fair comparison looks at the total cost of ownership over several years.

## The costs beyond the subscription

When you compare quotes, add up:

1. **Implementation and setup** — one-time, sometimes large.
2. **Data migration** — moving your existing records in.
3. **Per-user or per-provider fees** — how the price scales as you grow.
4. **Add-on modules** — billing, patient engagement, reporting that cost extra.
5. **Integrations** — per-connection fees to clearinghouses, labs or other tools.
6. **Training and support** — included, or billed separately?
7. **Switching costs** — how hard and expensive it is to leave later.

## Build a like-for-like comparison

Vendors package differently, so normalize. List the capabilities your clinic actually needs, then price *the same capability set* from each vendor over a three-year horizon. A low base price with expensive add-ons can cost more than a higher all-inclusive price.

## Ask the uncomfortable questions

- What is **not** included at the quoted price?
- How has pricing changed for existing customers over time?
- What happens to the price at renewal?
- Can you **export your data** at no cost if you leave?

## The metric that matters

Convert everything to a three-year total cost of ownership, then weigh it against the time and revenue the software is supposed to save. Cheapest is not the goal; best value for your clinic's real needs is.
