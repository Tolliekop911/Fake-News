If you could track only one billing metric, clean-claim rate would be a strong candidate. It captures, in a single number, how much friction stands between finishing a visit and getting paid for it.

## What it measures

Clean-claim rate is the share of claims accepted on first submission without needing edits, resubmission or additional information. A high rate means claims sail through; a low rate means staff time is burning on rework and cash is arriving late.

## Why it matters more than it seems

Every claim that isn't clean costs twice: once in delayed payment, and again in the labor to fix and resubmit it. A low clean-claim rate quietly inflates administrative cost and stretches days in accounts receivable. Improving it improves cash flow *and* frees staff.

## How to calculate it

In plain terms: the number of claims accepted on first pass divided by total claims submitted, over a period. Decide your exact definition (what counts as "clean") and apply it consistently so the trend is meaningful.

## Where unclean claims come from

- Eligibility or authorization not verified before the visit.
- Coding errors or mismatches with documentation.
- Missing or incorrect patient/payer information.
- Formatting or field errors the clearinghouse rejects.

Most of these are front-end problems — caught cheaply before submission, expensively after denial.

## How to improve it

1. **Verify eligibility and authorization up front.**
2. **Scrub claims** against payer rules before they go out.
3. **Track denial reasons** and fix the recurring causes, not just the individual claims.
4. **Watch the trend**, not a single month.
