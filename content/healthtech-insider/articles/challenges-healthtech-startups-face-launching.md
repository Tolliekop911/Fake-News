Launching software is hard anywhere. Launching healthcare software means doing it under constraints that most startups never face. Founders who understand these constraints early waste less time and money.

## Compliance is table stakes, not a feature

In most industries you can ship a minimum viable product and add security later. In healthcare, data protection and privacy obligations apply from the first real user. That raises the cost of the earliest version and slows the first launch — but it is not optional.

## The sales cycle is long and cautious

Clinics are risk-averse buyers, and for good reason: switching software disrupts patient care and revenue. Decisions involve owners, clinicians and billing staff, and they take months. A startup's runway has to survive that cycle.

## Trust has to be earned, not claimed

New vendors have no track record. References, transparent documentation, and a credible founding story do more than a feature list. Many health-tech startups underestimate how much of early growth is relationship work.

## Migration is a make-or-break moment

A clinic's existing data is both the biggest switching cost and the biggest source of anxiety. Startups that make migration smooth and trustworthy remove the single largest objection to switching.

## What tends to work

Founders who succeed usually:

- pick a **narrow, underserved niche** rather than competing broadly;
- treat compliance and security as core product, not overhead;
- invest heavily in onboarding and support;
- prove, with evidence, every claim they make.
