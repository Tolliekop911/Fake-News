Young healthcare software companies often have genuinely better products than incumbents — and they also have the strongest incentive to overstate. As a buyer, your job is to test the claims, not the enthusiasm.

## Ask for the evidence behind each number

Any claim of accuracy, time savings, adoption or revenue impact should come with a source. "Clinics save hours a week" means little without knowing how it was measured, on what sample, and compared to what.

- **Time savings:** measured how, over what period, versus what baseline?
- **Accuracy:** on what inputs, judged by whom?
- **Adoption / customers:** real paying customers, or pilots and sign-ups?

## Separate available from planned

Demos often blend shipping features with the roadmap. Ask directly: *is this live today for all customers, in beta, or planned?* Get it in writing.

## Talk to real customers

A startup should be able to connect you with current users in a similar setting. Ask those users what went wrong, not just what went right.

## Test it on your own data

A controlled trial on your real workflows tells you more than any case study. Define success before you start, and measure it.

## Watch for red flags

- Reluctance to explain methodology behind a claim.
- "Everyone is switching to us" with no verifiable references.
- Comparisons to named competitors with no stated basis.
