Few things cost outpatient physical therapy practices more quietly than authorization problems. A patient keeps coming, the clinic keeps treating, and then a claim is denied because the authorized visits ran out three sessions ago. Understanding the authorization process is the first defense.

## What an authorization actually is

An authorization is a payer's advance approval to deliver a defined amount of care — often a set number of visits within a date range, tied to a plan of care. It is a promise to pay *if* the conditions are met. Miss a condition and the promise evaporates.

## Where authorizations go wrong

Most losses come from a handful of recurring failures:

1. **Starting care before approval** is in hand.
2. **Running past the authorized visit count** without re-authorizing.
3. **Expiring date ranges** — approved visits that are never used in time.
4. **Mismatches** between the authorized plan of care and what was delivered.

Each of these is predictable, which means each is preventable.

## Building an authorization safety net

A reliable process has three parts:

- **Verify and authorize before the first visit.** No exceptions for "we'll catch up later."
- **Track remaining visits and expiry dates** where the front desk and clinicians can see them.
- **Trigger re-authorization early** — before the last authorized visit, not after.

> The goal is simple: no patient should ever be treated on an authorization that has quietly run out.

## Where software helps

Good practice-management software surfaces remaining visits and warns before authorizations lapse, turning a manual tracking chore into an automatic alert. That is exactly the kind of workflow worth evaluating when you choose an EHR.
