The "8-minute rule" governs how many billable units a clinic can charge for timed therapy services. It sounds simple and trips up practices constantly. This is a plain-language overview — but always confirm the current official thresholds and payer-specific rules before applying them.

> **Verify before relying on this:** billing rules change and vary by payer. Confirm current CMS guidance and each payer's policy. Nothing here is billing advice.

## The basic idea

Some therapy services are billed in time-based units; others are billed per session regardless of time. The 8-minute rule applies to the **timed** services. In broad terms, you total the minutes of timed treatment and convert them into units, where a minimum number of minutes is required to bill the first unit and additional units accrue as the total time crosses defined thresholds.

## Why clinics get it wrong

- **Mixing timed and untimed services** without separating the minutes.
- **Rounding casually** instead of applying the thresholds.
- **Documentation that doesn't support the minutes billed** — the most dangerous error in an audit.

## Documentation is the real protection

The number of units you bill must be defensible from your notes. That means recording the minutes of each timed intervention, not just the total visit length. If the documentation and the units don't match, the units lose.

## How software reduces the risk

Therapy-specific documentation tools can total timed minutes, apply the current thresholds, and flag mismatches before a claim goes out — reducing both lost revenue and audit exposure.
